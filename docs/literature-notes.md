# Literature verification and citation corrections

Checked November 11, 2026. This file records source verification and proposed bibliography text; it does **not** change the manuscript. The manual wording queue is in `docs/manual-revisions.md`.

## Verified core sources

**Caves, Whinston, and Hurwitz (1991).** *Patent Expiration, Entry, and Competition in the U.S. Pharmaceutical Industry.* Brookings Papers on Economic Activity: Microeconomics, 1991, 1–66. The original Brookings PDF was downloaded and read. It studies thirty drugs losing patent protection during 1976–1987, including branded and generic prices, market shares, quantities, and advertising. It supplies a directly relevant foundation for the discussion of entry. It does not make the thesis's cross-drug regression a causal entry design.

Primary source: https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/1991/01/1991_bpeamicro_caves.pdf

**Reiffen and Ward (2005).** *Generic Drug Industry Dynamics.* Review of Economics and Statistics, 87(1), 37–49. Authors, title, year, journal, and pages were verified against the publisher-deposited Crossref record. The limited proposed sentence says the paper examines generic-industry entry and pricing; no numerical finding from the paper is inserted. Full-text FTC working-paper URLs tried during this pass returned 404, so a full-text empirical finding is not claimed as newly verified here.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1162/0034653053327694

**Grabowski and Vernon (1992).** *Brand Loyalty, Entry, and Price Competition in Pharmaceuticals after the 1984 Drug Act.* Journal of Law and Economics, 35(2), 331–350. Bibliographic details were verified against the publisher-deposited Crossref record. **The correct DOI ends in 467257; 467259 is a different paper.** No unverified numerical effect is proposed.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/467257

**Conrad and Lutter (2019).** *Generic Competition and Drug Prices: New Evidence Linking Greater Generic Competition and Lower Generic Drug Prices.* U.S. Food and Drug Administration, December. The original FDA report was downloaded and read. Its opening results report generic average manufacturer prices 54% below the pre-entry brand price with two competitors and 79% below with four. The sample has initial generic entry in 2015–2017. These are manufacturer-price comparisons, not patient payments. The report also uses invoice prices, which produce different estimates. Do not combine the measures or describe “54% lower” as “54% of.” This source should replace the manuscript's unsupported August 2022 attribution and 55%/33%/13% sequence.

Primary source: https://www.fda.gov/media/133509/download

## Corrections to existing citations

**Janssen Daalen et al. (2021).** *Determinants of drug prices: a systematic review of comparison studies.* BMJ Open, 11(7), e046917. Publisher-deposited metadata and the authors' abstract were checked. Use the family name **Janssen Daalen**, not just Daalen, and **2021**, not 2016. The review distinguishes retail prices from the much sparser evidence on net prices. It supports discussion of associations with generic market share, purchasing, and price regulation, not a claim that the competition literature barely exists.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-046917
Author manuscript record: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34266841

**Keyhani, Diener-West, and Powe (2006).** *Are Development Times for Pharmaceuticals Increasing or Decreasing?* Health Affairs, 25(2), 461–468. Publisher-deposited metadata and the authors' abstract were checked. The study examines development times, not a broad correlation between R&D expenditure and prices. Its findings concern clinical-trial and regulatory-review periods among 168 drugs; the proposed replacement limits the attribution accordingly.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1377/hlthaff.25.2.461
Author abstract: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16522587

**Kesselheim, Avorn, and Sarpatwari (2016).** *The High Cost of Prescription Drugs in the United States: Origins and Prospects for Reform.* JAMA, 316(8), 858–871. The authors' abstract and citation record were checked. This is a review of pricing institutions and potential reforms, not a regression benchmark that the thesis can “confirm.” It addresses market exclusivity, generic competition, and payer bargaining.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.11237
Author abstract: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27552619

**Moore et al. (2018).** *Estimated Costs of Pivotal Trials for Novel Therapeutic Agents Approved by the US Food and Drug Administration, 2015–2016.* JAMA Internal Medicine, 178(11), 1451–1457. Publisher-deposited metadata and the authors' abstract were checked. The observations are **138 pivotal efficacy trials supporting 59 drugs**, not 59 complete drug-development programs. Their median estimated trial cost is $19.0 million. The low end is **less than $5 million**, not a $5 million minimum. The high end is $346.8 million. Keep these distinct from DiMasi's development-cost estimates.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2018.3931
Author article record: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30264133

**DiMasi, Grabowski, and Hansen (2016).** *Innovation in the Pharmaceutical Industry: New Estimates of R&D Costs.* Journal of Health Economics, 47, 20–33. Publisher-deposited metadata and the authors' abstract were checked. The reported average out-of-pocket estimate is $1,395 million; capitalized **pre-approval** cost is $2,558 million; adding post-approval R&D yields $2,870 million, all in 2013 dollars. Capitalized and cash costs are distinct concepts. The manuscript's narrower $1,098 million pre-human estimate was not checked against the full article during this pass; omit that detail or verify the full table before retaining it.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhealeco.2016.01.012
Author abstract: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26928437

**Mulcahy and Kareddy (2022).** *Prescription Drug Supply Chains: An Overview of Stakeholders and Relationships.* RAND Health Quarterly, 9(3), article 7. Publisher-deposited metadata and the authors' abstract were checked. **2022 is valid for the journal version**; the underlying RAND report appeared in 2021. Specify which version is cited. This supports the supply-chain illustration, but does not establish that the inferred Cost Plus measure includes every manufacturing-related cost shown in that illustration.

Journal DOI: https://doi.org/10.7249/rhq-09-03-07
Author article record: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35837523

**Rajkumar (2020).** *The High Cost of Prescription Drugs: Causes and Solutions.* Blood Cancer Journal, 10, article 71. Publication metadata was checked. The source is contextual commentary; the revised empirical claims should not be framed as confirming its causal explanation. The introductory global and U.S. spending totals have not been independently refreshed, and if retained must remain clearly attributed to this historical source.

Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41408-020-0338-x
Author article record: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32576816
